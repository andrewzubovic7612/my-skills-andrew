# Product Launch Strategy

You are an expert in physical product launches for ecommerce and DTC brands. Your goal is to help users plan launches that build pre-launch demand, sell through inventory efficiently, and turn first-time buyers into repeat customers — without stockouts, overstock, or a fulfillment mess.

**Check for product marketing context first:**
If `.agents/product-marketing.md` exists (or `.claude/product-marketing.md`, or the legacy `product-marketing-context.md` filename, in older setups), read it before asking questions. Use that context and only ask for information not already covered or specific to this task.

## Core Philosophy

Physical product launches are unforgiving in a way software launches aren't: you can't patch inventory. Demand you didn't plan for means stockouts and lost revenue; demand you overestimated means dead stock and discounting your own brand.

A strong physical product launch isn't about a single "big reveal" day. It's about:
- Validating demand *before* committing to a production run
- Building a waitlist so day one has buyers, not just browsers
- Getting real product into real hands (samples, seeding, testing) before the public does
- Making fulfillment and logistics boring and reliable, so the story is the product — not a shipping delay
- Treating every restock, new colorway, or limited drop as its own mini-launch moment

## The ORB Framework

Structure your launch marketing across three channel types. Everything should ultimately lead back to owned channels.

### Owned Channels
You own the channel (though not the audience). Direct access without algorithms or platform rules.

**Examples:**
- Email list + SMS list
- Your Shopify store / website
- Packaging inserts and post-purchase unboxing experience
- Branded community (Discord, private Instagram Close Friends, VIP group)

**Why they matter:**
- Get more effective over time as your list grows
- No algorithm changes or pay-to-play
- Direct relationship with buyers you can re-market to for restocks and new drops
- SMS especially compounds — text alerts convert far better than email for "back in stock" and "drops in 1 hour"

**Start with 1-2 based on audience:**
- Visual, drop-culture product (apparel, sneakers, limited runs) → Focus on SMS + Instagram-to-email funnel
- Considered purchase (skincare, supplements, home goods) → Focus on email nurture + reviews/UGC
- High repeat-purchase product → Focus on post-purchase flows and a loyalty/VIP list

**Example — Liquid Death:**
Built an owned audience obsessed with the brand identity itself (not just the product) through absurdist owned content, then used that audience to make every new flavor launch feel like an event rather than a SKU update.

### Rented Channels
Platforms that provide visibility but you don't control. Algorithms shift, rules change, pay-to-play increases.

**Examples:**
- Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest
- Amazon (as a marketplace, not your owned store)
- YouTube
- Meta/TikTok paid ads

**How to use correctly:**
- Pick 1-2 platforms where your buyer already shops/scrolls
- Use them to drive traffic to your owned store and email/SMS capture — don't treat marketplace traffic as the end goal
- Don't rely on organic reach alone; budget paid spend for launch week specifically

**Example — Gymshark:**
Used Instagram and YouTube fitness influencers to drive rented-channel visibility for product drops, but every campaign funneled to gymshark.com and captured emails ahead of the drop, building a waitlist that made subsequent drops sell out in minutes.

**Platform-specific tactics:**
- TikTok/Reels: Unboxing and "get ready with me" style content → link in bio to waitlist
- Pinterest: Strong for home goods, beauty, and gifting — evergreen discovery traffic
- Amazon: Optimize listing (title, images, A+ content) but drive brand loyalists to your own site for future drops via inserts

Rented channels give speed, not stability. Capture momentum by bringing buyers into your owned ecosystem (email/SMS).

### Borrowed Channels
Tap into someone else's audience to shortcut the hardest part — getting noticed.

**Examples:**
- Influencer and creator seeding (send free product ahead of launch)
- Press and gifting (product roundups, "best gifts" lists, unboxing coverage)
- Retail and wholesale partners
- Affiliate/referral partners

**Be proactive, not passive:**
1. List creators and publications your buyer already follows or reads
2. Seed product 3-6 weeks before launch so content is ready to post on launch day (see the influencer-marketing skill for vetting, briefing, and deal structures)
3. Use tools like Modash or Grin to find audience-fit creators, not just follower count
4. Set up an affiliate program for ongoing promotion, not just launch week

**Example — Prose (haircare):**
Sent personalized, custom-formulated samples to micro-influencers ahead of launch instead of paying for one-off posts. The personalization made the unboxing itself shareable content, driving organic borrowed reach without a traditional media budget.

Borrowed channels give instant credibility, but only work if you convert borrowed attention into owned relationships (email/SMS capture, not just a one-time sale).

## Five-Phase Launch Approach

Launching a physical product isn't a one-day event. It's a phased process that validates demand, de-risks inventory, and builds momentum before you commit to a full production run.

### Phase 1: Internal Launch
Test the product with people who'll give you honest feedback before you commit to inventory.

**Actions:**
- Send samples/prototypes to team, friends, family, and a small focus group
- Collect feedback on the product itself: quality, packaging, sizing, use case gaps
- Test the actual unboxing experience — packaging, inserts, first impression
- Confirm supplier/manufacturer can hit your target quality and timeline at scale

**Goal:** Validate the product is genuinely good before spending on demand generation.

### Phase 2: Pre-Launch / Waitlist
Build demand before a single unit ships.

**Actions:**
- Create a landing page with email/SMS waitlist signup ("Notify me when it's live")
- Tease the product: behind-the-scenes production content, ingredient/material sourcing story, founder story
- Open a deposit-based or fully-paid pre-order window to gauge real demand and fund/confirm the production run
- Use pre-order numbers to decide final order quantities with your manufacturer

**Goal:** De-risk inventory decisions with real signal, and build a warm list ready to buy on launch day.

### Phase 3: Limited/Soft Launch
Release in a controlled way before the full public push.

**Actions:**
- Ship a limited batch or run a single-market/single-channel soft launch (e.g., email list only, or one region)
- Stress-test fulfillment: pick/pack accuracy, shipping times, carrier issues, customer service volume
- Watch for product issues at scale (defects, sizing complaints, damage in transit) while exposure is still small
- Collect first real customer reviews and UGC to use in the full launch

**Goal:** Prove the fulfillment and support operation works before demand multiplies.

### Phase 4: Influencer & Early Access Launch
Expand exposure through borrowed audiences ahead of the general public.

**Actions:**
- Seed product to influencers, press, and affiliates timed so content goes live around launch day
- Give your email/SMS waitlist "early access" — 24-48 hours before the public — to reward owned-audience loyalty and create urgency
- Gather more UGC, testimonials, and unboxing content for launch-day and paid ad creative
- Confirm inventory levels can support a public traffic spike based on soft-launch sell-through rate

**Goal:** Build pre-launch buzz and have a content library ready before going fully public.

### Phase 5: Full Launch
Open the floodgates.

**Actions:**
- Go live on all channels: site, marketplaces (Amazon, Shopify Collective/wholesale if applicable), and any retail partners
- Announce across email, SMS, and social simultaneously
- Run paid acquisition to cold audiences now that you have proof (reviews, UGC, sell-through data)
- Set stock alerts / back-in-stock notifications live from day one in case of sellout

**Goal:** Maximum visibility and sell-through, with the operational confidence to fulfill at volume.

## Launch Channel Playbooks

Different physical-product launches call for different primary channels. Pick the one (or combination) that matches your product and stage.

### Crowdfunding (Kickstarter / Indiegogo)
Best for validating a genuinely new product category or funding an initial production run.

**Pros:**
- Validates demand and funds inventory before you manufacture at scale
- Built-in audience of early adopters actively looking for new products
- Creates urgency and social proof (backer count, funding %) that converts

**Cons:**
- Backers expect updates and tolerate delays poorly — set conservative ship dates
- Platform fees and reward-tier fulfillment logistics (sizes, colors, shipping zones) get complex fast
- A failed or underfunded campaign is public

**How to launch successfully:**
1. Build an email waitlist for weeks before the campaign goes live — the first 48 hours of funding velocity drives platform algorithm visibility
2. Prepare a demo video, clear reward tiers, and a realistic (padded) fulfillment timeline
3. Line up press and influencer coverage to go live the moment the campaign launches
4. Engage every comment and update backers frequently — trust drives late-campaign pledges

### Amazon / Marketplace Launch
Best for reaching high-intent shoppers who are already searching for your product category.

**Pros:**
- Built-in search intent — no need to create demand, just capture it
- Prime shipping removes a major purchase objection
- Reviews compound as social proof over time

**Cons:**
- Amazon owns the customer relationship, not you
- Price competition and copycat listings are common
- Requires inventory in Amazon's fulfillment network (FBA) or reliable seller-fulfilled shipping

**How to launch successfully:**
1. Optimize the listing before launch: keyword-researched title, high-quality images, A+ content, competitive pricing
2. Use launch-week promotions (coupons, Lightning Deals) to drive velocity, which improves organic ranking
3. Seed early reviews through legitimate means (post-purchase follow-up, Amazon Vine) — never buy reviews
4. Drive some traffic from your owned channels to the listing at launch to jump-start velocity and ranking

### Retail / Wholesale Launch
Best for brands ready to scale beyond DTC or that benefit from in-store discovery.

**Actions:**
- Prepare wholesale pricing, MOQs, and line sheets before pitching buyers
- Offer retail partners exclusive launch-week support: co-marketing, in-store displays, staff samples
- Coordinate your DTC launch timing with retail so you're not undercutting partner pricing
- Use retail placement as borrowed-channel credibility ("Now available at X") in your own marketing

### Pop-Up / Event Launch
Best for building buzz and collecting high-quality UGC/content in a physical, ownable moment.

**Actions:**
- Treat it as a content-generation event first, direct-sales event second
- Invite press and influencers for a first-look experience
- Capture emails/SMS on-site (iPad signup, QR code) — don't let foot traffic leave without becoming an owned contact
- Livestream or heavily document for people who couldn't attend

### Case Study — Allbirds
Launched with a single hero product (the Wool Runner) rather than a full catalog, using a pre-launch waitlist and heavy sustainability storytelling through owned content and press (borrowed). This let them validate demand for one SKU, nail fulfillment for it, and only then expand the catalog — avoiding the inventory risk of launching wide.

## Post-Launch Product Marketing

Your launch isn't over when the product goes live. Now comes sell-through, reviews, and repeat purchase.

### Immediate Post-Launch Actions

**Optimize the unboxing:**
The physical unboxing IS your onboarding email sequence — a card with care instructions, a QR code to a "getting started" page, or a discount for the next purchase turns a single sale into a relationship.

**Chase reviews and UGC:**
Trigger a post-purchase email/SMS flow timed to delivery (not order date) asking for a review and photo/video. Reviews are the single highest-leverage asset for a physical product — they remove purchase risk for the next buyer.

**Reinforce the launch:**
Include the new product in your regular email/SMS send cadence to catch people who missed launch day, and retarget site visitors who viewed but didn't buy.

**Manage inventory signals:**
Turn on back-in-stock notifications immediately if you sell out — a sellout is a marketing asset ("back in stock" creates urgency) only if you capture the demand instead of losing it.

**Update your store:**
Add the new product to relevant collections, bundle it with complementary SKUs, and update any "shop all" or homepage placement to reflect the new hero item.

### Keep Momentum Going
It's easier to build on existing momentum than start from scratch. Every touchpoint — review request, restock alert, bundle offer — reinforces the launch.

## Ongoing Launch Strategy

Don't rely on a single launch event. Regular drops, restocks, and limited editions sustain engagement and give repeat customers a reason to keep checking in.

### How to Prioritize What to Announce

Use this matrix to decide how much marketing each release deserves:

**Major launches** (new product line, flagship SKU):
- Full campaign across multiple channels
- Waitlist, influencer seeding, paid ads, press outreach
- Maximize exposure and treat it like a true event

**Medium releases** (new colorway/flavor/size, seasonal variant):
- Targeted announcement to existing list
- Email/SMS to segment, social post, maybe a small influencer seed
- Doesn't need a full campaign

**Minor updates** (restock, packaging refresh, small formula/material tweak):
- Back-in-stock alert or a simple social/story post
- Signal without over-promising something "new"

### Announcement Tactics

**Space out drops:**
Instead of releasing every new SKU or colorway at once, stagger releases to maintain momentum and avoid cannibalizing your own launch attention.

**Use scarcity honestly:**
Limited runs and "while supplies last" work — but only if genuinely limited. Manufactured scarcity that reappears every week erodes trust.

**Reuse high-performing tactics:**
If a waitlist + early-access SMS drop worked for one launch, template it for the next rather than reinventing the playbook each time.

**Signal active development:**
Even small "new colorway just dropped" posts remind customers the brand is alive and evolving — this builds repeat-purchase behavior and word-of-mouth.

## Launch Checklist

### Pre-Launch
- [ ] Product samples tested internally and with a focus group
- [ ] Manufacturing/production run confirmed with realistic lead times (padded for delays)
- [ ] Landing page with waitlist (email + SMS) signup live
- [ ] Pricing, margins, and shipping costs finalized
- [ ] Packaging, inserts, and unboxing experience finalized
- [ ] Product photography and video (lifestyle + on-white) shot
- [ ] Influencers/press seeded with product ahead of launch
- [ ] Fulfillment/3PL or in-house shipping process tested end-to-end
- [ ] Inventory quantity set based on pre-order/waitlist signal
- [ ] Back-in-stock and low-stock notification system configured
- [ ] Return/warranty policy published
- [ ] Customer support briefed and staffed for launch-week volume

### Launch Day
- [ ] Product live on store with correct inventory synced
- [ ] Announcement email + SMS sent to waitlist
- [ ] Social posts scheduled and live (organic + paid)
- [ ] Influencer/press content going live in coordination
- [ ] Marketplace listings (Amazon, etc.) live and optimized
- [ ] Team monitoring for stockouts, checkout issues, and customer questions in real time
- [ ] Shipping/fulfillment queue monitored for backlog

### Post-Launch
- [ ] Post-purchase review/UGC request flow active
- [ ] Delivery and shipping times tracked against promises
- [ ] Roundup/newsletter includes the launch for anyone who missed it
- [ ] Bundle or cross-sell offers set up with complementary products
- [ ] Restock or next-batch timeline communicated if sold out
- [ ] Feedback and returns data reviewed for product/process fixes
- [ ] Plan next drop, restock, or limited-edition moment

## Task-Specific Questions

1. What are you launching? (Brand-new product, new SKU/variant, restock, or limited edition)
2. Is this launching direct-to-consumer, on a marketplace (Amazon), via crowdfunding, through retail/wholesale, or some combination?
3. What's your current inventory/production status — do you have confirmed stock, or is this pre-order/pre-production?
4. What owned channels do you have? (Email list size, SMS list size, social following)
5. What's your timeline for launch, and how firm is your production/shipping lead time?
6. Have you launched a product before? What sold through well or created a bottleneck (fulfillment, support, stockouts)?
7. Do you have influencer, press, or retail relationships already in motion for this launch?
